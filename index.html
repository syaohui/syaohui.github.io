Introduction

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Statistics at Stony Brook University. I study how turbulent fluctuations shape populations of cloud droplets, using stochastic differential equations, Monte Carlo simulation, and information-theoretic causal inference. From 2024 to 2026 I was a research assistant at Brookhaven National Laboratory. I am looking for postdoctoral and research scientist positions starting in 2027.

Full biography

Research

My work asks why cloud droplet size distributions are broader than classical condensation theory predicts, and what drives what inside the coupled system of turbulence, water vapor, and droplets. I build minimal stochastic models of droplet growth, derive what can be derived exactly, and test those results against large Monte Carlo ensembles. A second line of work develops causal discovery methods for systems with feedback and unobserved drivers.
