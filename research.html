Research Topics

Updated October 2026

Full publication list on Google Scholar and the publications page.

Research summary. My research studies turbulence-microphysics interactions in clouds with the tools of stochastic analysis and statistical inference. Droplets grow and evaporate in a supersaturation field that fluctuates with finite memory, and those fluctuations broaden the droplet size distribution. I ask how the memory and intensity of the fluctuations set that broadening, how to write minimal models that can be solved or simulated with controlled error, and how to measure what causes what in systems where the variables feed back on each other. Newer work carries these questions to turbulence simulations and to AI agents that design and check them.

Stochastic condensation with finite-memory fluctuations

The McGraw-Liu drift-diffusion model treats saturation fluctuations as white noise, which holds only when they decorrelate quickly. Replacing that noise with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process for the saturation ratio gives a coupled Langevin system that covers the full range of correlation times and recovers the white-noise model as a limit. Monte Carlo ensembles of 105 droplets show that spectral broadening grows with both the correlation time and the intensity of the fluctuations.

Information flow and causality in stochastic models

Building the Liang-Kleeman information-flow formalism into the condensation model gives a closed form for the rate at which saturation fluctuations inform droplet size, together with its white-noise and frozen-noise limits. An ensemble estimator that uses covariances across droplets at each instant reproduces the analytical flow, stays near zero in the reverse direction, and identifies a causal direction that the symmetric correlation coefficient cannot. The causal coupling weakens as the size distribution broadens.

Maximum entropy and Langevin dynamics of droplet spectra

Maximum-entropy systems theory and Langevin stochastic condensation, applied to the same conservation constraint, predict Weibull spectra of different orders. The difference comes from the choice of size variable. Generalizing the Langevin model to any conserved moment order needs one hypothesis, that each droplet exchanges the conserved budget at a size-independent rate, and Monte Carlo runs for orders 1 to 6 reproduce the predicted narrowing of the spectrum.

Entrainment-mixing

Dry air entrained into a cloud reduces droplet number, liquid water, or both, depending on how fast mixing is compared with evaporation. A stochastic condensation model with explicit homogeneous and extreme-inhomogeneous mixing pathways, and a scheme that moves continuously between them, provides a benchmark for entrainment-mixing parameterizations.

Causal discovery with feedback and unobserved drivers

Standard causal discovery assumes an acyclic graph and that every common cause is measured, and turbulent clouds violate both. This methodology targets partial ancestral graphs over a lag window under sigma-separation, uses nearest-neighbor conditional mutual information tests, and separates nonlinearity from nonstationarity with surrogate-calibrated diagnostics. For causal strength it proposes the relative entropy rate between path measures with one drift dependence removed, which has a closed form by Girsanov's theorem.

Causal learning on turbulence simulations

Small random perturbations added to a turbulence simulation turn one run into many randomized experiments, so cause and effect can be estimated the way a controlled trial estimates a treatment effect. The data come from pseudo-spectral Navier-Stokes solvers for 2D and 3D turbulence and convection, checked against exact solutions and linear stability theory. On 2D turbulence the method labeled all 16 causal links correctly, and its confidence intervals held the true effect in 127 of 128 tests.

AI agents for simulation-driven discovery

Large fluid simulations are expensive, and the hard part is telling real physical effects from numerical or statistical artifacts. I am building an LLM-based AI agent for two-fluid flow simulations with the FronTier solver. It proposes hypotheses, ranks candidate runs by expected information per unit of compute, and revises its explanation when a forecast fails. Its verification controller reached 0.02% error on a Taylor-Green test case with a known answer.
